# My journey: from engineer to delivery leader

*2008 – today · How the scope of what I lead has grown, and what each stage taught me.*

I've spent my whole career in data: first building it, then leading the people who build it, then owning the commercial and client outcomes around it. This page is the honest version of that path, including what I learned the hard way.

## Growth at a glance

| | 2008 – 2012 | 2012 – 2017 | 2017 – 2022 | 2023 – today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Role** | ETL Developer → Senior Developer | Project Consultant | Technical Project Manager | Senior Technical Project Manager |
| **Leading** | My own deliverables | Delivery teams across EMEA | A multi-year program across [N] pods | A 29-person track (8 onsite, 21 offshore) |
| **Scope** | Data integration & reporting | End-to-end integration systems | 500-application portfolio | $15M, 4-year delivery track |
| **Accountable for** | Quality of my pipelines and reports | Delivery and client satisfaction | Program outcomes and savings | Plan, forecast, billing and margin |

<div class="tl-years">2008 – 2012</div>

### Learning the craft

<div class="tl-sub">ETL Developer → Senior Developer · IT services organization</div>

<div class="chips"><span class="chip">ETL</span><span class="chip">SQL</span><span class="chip">PL/SQL</span><span class="chip">Shell scripting</span><span class="chip">Tableau</span></div>

I started in 2008 as an ETL developer, building data integration pipelines in SQL, PL/SQL and shell scripts, and the Tableau reports that sat on top of them. Over four years I grew into a senior developer role, trusted with the more complex integrations end to end.

Working at both ends, loading the data and building the reports people actually used, taught me how the whole chain fits together, and how a small problem upstream turns into a wrong number in front of a business user.

<div class="tl-lesson" markdown>**Leadership lesson:** Know the work you'll one day lead. Having built pipelines and reports myself, I can still ask the right question in a review, spot an unrealistic estimate, and earn credibility with engineers.</div>
</div>

<div class="tl-item" markdown>
<div class="tl-years">2012 – 2017</div>

### Leading delivery in front of the client

<div class="tl-sub">Project Consultant · Tata Consultancy Services, for a leading biopharma company · London, UK</div>

<div class="chips"><span class="chip">Enterprise data integration</span><span class="chip">Reporting at scale</span><span class="chip">Onsite–offshore</span><span class="chip">Stakeholder management</span></div>

Five years onsite in London as the client-facing consultant for a leading biopharma company: owning the product backlog and end-to-end delivery of enterprise data integration and reporting, including dashboards used by 2,000+ sales people across EMEA. We grew the account by 10% while keeping customer satisfaction above 95%.

This is where I learned to run a project *with* the client in the room: managing expectations, saying no with data, and bridging an onsite team and an offshore team across time zones.

<div class="tl-lesson" markdown>**Leadership lesson:** Trust is built by bad news delivered early. Clients forgive slips; they don't forgive surprises.</div>
</div>

<div class="tl-item" markdown>
<div class="tl-years">2017 – 2022</div>

### Running a program at scale

<div class="tl-sub">Technical Project Manager, Big Data & Analytics · Tata Consultancy Services, for a global medical device OEM · Gurgaon, India</div>

<div class="chips"><span class="chip">Portfolio rationalization</span><span class="chip">Legacy decommissioning</span><span class="chip">Big data modernization</span><span class="chip">Agile at scale</span></div>

I led a five-year program to decommission 500 legacy applications, saving about $12M. We spent two years analysing a portfolio of [~1,500] applications to decide what could safely go, then delivered in waves: a 20-app pilot, then waves that grew to 120 apps each as we turned the process into a factory.

In parallel, we moved legacy architecture to modern big-data platforms and deployed 100+ automated pipelines.

<div class="tl-lesson" markdown>**Leadership lesson:** Plan around the real constraint. In our case it wasn't engineering capacity; it was legal and business approvals. Once we planned around sign-off throughput, the waves ran on time. And the pilot is for learning, not for volume.</div>
</div>

<div class="tl-item now" markdown>
<div class="tl-years">2023 – today</div>

### Owning outcomes: data, AI and commercials

<div class="tl-sub">Senior Technical Project Manager · Tata Consultancy Services, for a leading insurance firm · Remote from Grenoble, France</div>

<div class="chips"><span class="chip">Databricks lakehouse</span><span class="chip">AI-ready data</span><span class="chip">Commercial ownership</span><span class="chip">Customer Journey Analytics</span></div>

I lead a $15M, four-year delivery track with 29 people (8 onsite, 21 offshore). We built an enterprise Databricks lakehouse processing millions of daily customer interactions, designed as the foundation for analytics, machine learning and LLM use cases.

My role now goes beyond delivery: I own the plan, the monthly revenue forecast, billing, change control and margin, and I'm part of shaping the contract renewal.

<div class="tl-lesson" markdown>**Leadership lesson:** A project is a commercial commitment first and a delivery plan second. Most delivery problems trace back to how the deal was estimated, so I start managing risk at sales handover, not at kickoff.</div>
</div>

## What's next

I'm now investing deliberately in AI: learning in public on this site, building my first Claude agent, and applying 18 years of delivery experience to a question most organizations are struggling with: how do you take AI from a demo to something that runs reliably, safely and profitably?

## How my leadership has changed

- **From output to outcomes:** from "did we build it right" to "did it deliver the business value and the margin we promised".
- **From managing tasks to designing systems:** entry gates, factories and forecasts that let a team run without me in every decision.
- **From my team to the whole deal:** clients, partners, third parties and finance are all part of delivery.
- **From certainty to evidence:** especially with AI, I now plan for experimentation and decide on evidence, not on optimism.
